Nageshwar Jyotirlinga
Comprehensive Scriptural & Historical Record

1. Sacred Origins & Puranic Legend

According to the Shiva Purana, Lord Shiva manifested as a fiery pillar of light (Jyotirlinga) in the ancient forest of Darukavana. A demon named Daruka and his wife Daruki captured a devout follower named Supriya alongside local villagers. When Supriya led the captives in chanting Om Namah Shivaya, Shiva manifested as Nageshwar ("Lord of Serpents") to vanquish the demons and established his permanent presence at the site.

2. Subterranean Preservation Over Centuries

Situated on the Arabian Sea coastline of Dwarka, Gujarat, the temple's original upper stone structures were repeatedly damaged and submerged due to coastal erosion, siltation, and historical disruptions over centuries. While the ancient stone halls collapsed above ground, the core sacred artifact—a small, tri-mukhi (three-faced) stone Lingam—remained intact in an underground basement pit (-2.80 meters below ground level).

3. The 1996 Modern Reconstruction

In 1996, T-Series founder Gulshan Kumar and local temple trusts commissioned a complete rebuilding of the complex. Contractors built the modern reddish-orange Maru-Gurjara superstructure and an 85-foot (25m) seated Lord Shiva statue in the courtyard directly above the original site, preserving the subterranean Garbhagriha intact below.

4. Architectural Blueprint Alignment

The blueprint drawing sheet details this exact dual-layer arrangement: the upper ground level features the pillared Sabha Mandap and Maru-Gurjara spire, while a central stairwell allows devotees to descend into the preserved sub-grade pit to worship the ancient Lingam.
